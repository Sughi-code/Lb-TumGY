Plato — Phaedo

№ 18 in the general list of 120 great books

Annotation

The death-day of Socrates in dialogue with Simmias and Cebes: arguments for the soul's immortality from opposites, recollection and kinship to the unchanging, interrupted by calm rebukes of despair and the final dose of hemlock. Philosophy practiced as rehearsal for dying.

Historical Context

Set on the very day of the execution (399 BC) yet composed later, likely mid-380s; transmitted Pythagorean ideas into the mainstream and became the West's standard scene of the good death until Boethius.
