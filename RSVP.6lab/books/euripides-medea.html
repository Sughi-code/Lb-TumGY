Euripides — Medea

№ 11 in the general list of 120 great books

Annotation

Jason abandons Medea for a Corinthian princess; the betrayed sorceress kills the bride and bridegroom and, in the plays most terrible stroke, her own sons, escaping in the sun-god's chariot. Passion, exile and revenge are argued from the foreign woman's point of view.

Historical Context

Produced 431 BC, the year the Peloponnesian War began; Euripides' psychological realism and rhetorical women broke with Aeschylean solemnity, and his Medea has been restaged endlessly as a study of rage gendered and racialized.
