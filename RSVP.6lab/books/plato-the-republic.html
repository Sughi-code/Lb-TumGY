Plato — The Republic

№ 20 in the general list of 120 great books

Annotation

Justice in the soul and the city: the tripartite classes, philosopher-kings educated through mathematics and dialectic, the abolition of private property for guardians, the cave, line and divided city-soul analogies, and the critique of poetry that banishes the poets.

Historical Context

Composed c. 375 BC amid the decay of Athenian democracy and Socrates' legacy; it founded political philosophy, utopian writing and epistemology alike — Popper attacked it, Karl Popper named it open society's enemy, Marx and everyone else read it.
