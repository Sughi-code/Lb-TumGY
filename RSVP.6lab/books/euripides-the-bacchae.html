Euripides — The Bacchae

№ 12 in the general list of 120 great books

Annotation

Dionysus returns to Thebes as god and stranger; king Pentheus bans the new cult and spies on the maenads on Cithaeron, only to be torn apart by his own mother in divine madness. Reason and ecstasy collide without reconciliation, and piety is shown as neither safe nor simple.

Historical Context

Written in Macedon and produced posthumously in 405 BC; Nietzsche's Birth of Tragedy built its Apollonian/Dionysian pair on this play, and scholars still debate whether it endorses or dismantles the religion of its title-god.
