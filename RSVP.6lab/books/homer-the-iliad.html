Homer — The Iliad

№ 1 in the general list of 120 great books

Annotation

The oldest surviving masterpiece of Greek literature tells of a few weeks of the ten-year siege of Troy, centered on the wrath of Achilles — his quarrel with Agamemnon, withdrawal from battle, grief for Patroclus, and killing of Hector. The poem sets honour, mortality and compassion against the machinery of war, ending not with victory but with the ransom of Hector's body and a shared human grief.

Historical Context

Composed in oral-formulaic tradition around the 8th century BC from older songs about a Bronze-Age conflict; it was the schoolbook of ancient Greece, quoted by Plato, reworked by Virgil, and the yardstick of epic ever since — the founding text of the Western canon.
