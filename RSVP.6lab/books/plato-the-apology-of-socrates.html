Plato — The Apology of Socrates

№ 16 in the general list of 120 great books

Annotation

Socrates defends himself in court against Meletus, Anytus and Lycon: the examined life, the divine sign, refusal to flee or beg. He claims poverty as proof of integrity and warns Athens that silencing him wounds itself more than him.

Historical Context

Delivered 399 BC after the democracy's restoration; Plato, present at the trial, shaped testimony into philosophy's founding act of conscience — the model for every later defense of free thought, from Christian apologies to dissent trials.
