# Great Books — 120 Canon Annotations (English)

Source of truth for every book page: **Annotation** + **Historical Context**.
Format per entry: `## №N. Author — Title`, then `**Annotation:**` and `**Historical context:**`.

## №1. Homer — The Iliad
**Annotation:** The oldest surviving masterpiece of Greek literature tells of a few weeks of the ten-year siege of Troy, centered on the wrath of Achilles — his quarrel with Agamemnon, withdrawal from battle, grief for Patroclus, and killing of Hector. The poem sets honour, mortality and compassion against the machinery of war, ending not with victory but with the ransom of Hector's body and a shared human grief.
**Historical context:** Composed in oral-formulaic tradition around the 8th century BC from older songs about a Bronze-Age conflict; it was the schoolbook of ancient Greece, quoted by Plato, reworked by Virgil, and the yardstick of epic ever since — the founding text of the Western canon.

## №2. Homer — The Odyssey
**Annotation:** Ten years of wandering bring Odysseus home to Iliaca where suitors devour his estate; cunning, disguise, hospitality and reunion with Penelope replace battlefield glory as the poem's virtues. Its nested storytelling, descent to the underworld and faithful dog Argos created the template for every adventure-and-homecoming narrative.
**Historical context:** Dated to roughly the late 8th century BC, reflecting a world of Phoenician trade and colonial expansion; it became the second half of the Homeric curriculum that every educated Greek and Roman memorized, and its structure echoes through Dante, Joyce and cinema.

## №3. Aeschylus — The Oresteia (Trilogy: Agamemnon, The Libation Bearers, The Eumenides)
**Annotation:** The only complete Greek trilogy: Agamemnon returns from Troy and is murdered by Clytemnestra; Orestes avenges his father and is hunted by the Furies; at Athens Athena converts the blood-feud into a trial by jury, and the Erinyes become the Kindly Ones. Justice evolves from vengeance to law across three generations of cursed house.
**Historical context:** First performed in 458 BC, shortly after reforms transferred the powers of the old aristocratic Areopagus to democratic institutions; the drama sacralizes the Athenian court and marks the moment tragedy speaks directly to civic theory.

## №4. Aeschylus — Prometheus Bound
**Annotation:** Zeus chains the Titan who stole fire and gave humankind craft, speech and hope; Prometheus refuses submission, naming his crimes as benefits to mortals. The play stages defiant intellect against arbitrary power — the archetypal drama of the rebel for conscience' sake.
**Historical context:** Attributed to Aeschylus (5th century BC), though scholars long debated its authorship; rediscovered in the Romantic age, it became Shelley's Prometheus Unbound and the patron myth of every art that pits the individual against tyranny.

## №5. Sophocles — Oedipus Rex
**Annotation:** To save Thebes from plague, king Oedipus hunts the murderer of the former king and discovers himself: the child exposed at birth, who solved the Sphinx's riddle and fled his own oracle, has killed his father and married his mother. Sight and blindness, fate and self-knowledge cross in the tightest plot ever written.
**Historical context:** Performed c. 429 BC amid the plague and wars of Athens; Aristotle took it in the Poetics as the perfect tragedy — recognition and reversal fused — and Freud later borrowed its name, making it the most analyzed single text in Western culture.

## №6. Sophocles — Antigone
**Annotation:** Antigone buries her brother Polynices against Creon's edict, pleading the unwritten, eternal laws of the gods; the tyrant's stubbornness destroys his son, his bride-to-be and himself. Conscience versus state law is stated here for the first time with such force.
**Historical context:** Staged c. 441 BC in democratic Athens freshly debating obedience and decree; Hegel read it as the collision of two equally justified powers — family and state — and it still anchors every course on civil disobedience.

## №7. Sophocles — Oedipus at Colonus
**Annotation:** The blinded outcast arrives at Colonus near Athens, is taken in by Theseus, and dies not in humiliation but transfigured, promising his burial place as a blessing to the city that shelters him. Suffering is transfigured into sacred power in Sophocles' last play.
**Historical context:** Written during the final years of the Peloponnesian War and produced posthumously in 401 BC; its praise of Athens and its mysterious theology of the hero's tomb made it both civic testament and enigmatic summing-up of a life spent on fate.

## №8. Sophocles — Philoctetes
**Annotation:** Nine years marooned on Lemnos with a festering wound and Heracles' bow, Philoctetes is approached by Odysseus and Neoptolemus with orders to steal the arrows needed to take Troy; the young hero's revulsion at deceit becomes the play's moral pivot. Pain, pity and honesty versus military necessity.
**Historical context:** Produced 409 BC when Athenian realpolitik abroad clashed with professed civic ideals; Tennyson and many modern dramatists reworked its question — can a good end excuse a rotten means?

## №9. Herodotus — The Histories
**Annotation:** A vast inquiry into the Persian Wars that digresses endlessly and brilliantly: Egyptian customs, Scythian geography, Babylon's walls, the debates of Greeks and barbarians. Freedom versus despotism is framed as the cause worth fighting for, told with curiosity rather than condescension.
**Historical context:** Composed c. 440–425 BC by an author from Halicarnassus who recited parts at Panhellenic games; Cicero called him "the father of history," Thucydides implicitly rejected his method, and today he stands as the founder of ethnography too.

## №10. Thucydides — History of the Peloponnesian War
**Annotation:** An austere, evidence-driven account of the war between Athens and Sparta, punctuated by set speeches — Pericles' Funeral Oration, the Melian Dialogue's brutal realism, the disaster at Syracuse. Human nature, judged from within the plague-ridden city, is declared "a possession for all time."
**Historical context:** Written by an Athenian general exiled for losing Amphipolis (424 BC); refusing oracles and marvels, he invented political science and "realist" international relations, quoted by every strategist from Clausewitz to Kissinger.

## №11. Euripides — Medea
**Annotation:** Jason abandons Medea for a Corinthian princess; the betrayed sorceress kills the bride and bridegroom and, in the plays most terrible stroke, her own sons, escaping in the sun-god's chariot. Passion, exile and revenge are argued from the foreign woman's point of view.
**Historical context:** Produced 431 BC, the year the Peloponnesian War began; Euripides' psychological realism and rhetorical women broke with Aeschylean solemnity, and his Medea has been restaged endlessly as a study of rage gendered and racialized.

## №12. Euripides — The Bacchae
**Annotation:** Dionysus returns to Thebes as god and stranger; king Pentheus bans the new cult and spies on the maenads on Cithaeron, only to be torn apart by his own mother in divine madness. Reason and ecstasy collide without reconciliation, and piety is shown as neither safe nor simple.
**Historical context:** Written in Macedon and produced posthumously in 405 BC; Nietzsche's Birth of Tragedy built its Apollonian/Dionysian pair on this play, and scholars still debate whether it endorses or dismantles the religion of its title-god.

## №13. Euripides — Hippolytus
**Annotation:** Phaedra's nurse betrays her secret love for stepson Hippolytus, devoted to Artemis and virginity; a false accusation destroys the youth, and Poseidon's curse finishes what shame began. The goddess Artemis closes the play with the promise of future rites at Troezen.
**Historical context:** Produced 428 BC with prize-winning success; its anatomy of illicit desire, speech and slander made it a touchstone for psychoanalytic readings, and Racine's Phèdre rewrote it for the French classical stage.

## №14. Aristophanes — The Clouds
**Annotation:** Strepsiades, drowning in debts, sends his son to Socrates' Thinkery, where Argument Worse teaches him to cheat creditors; when the clever pupil beats his father, the old man torches the school. Comic assault on intellectual fashion, sophistry and the corruption of filial duty.
**Historical context:** Produced 423 BC, with the historical Socrates caricatured on stage — one root of the later charge that philosophy corrupted Athenian youth; the play remains our earliest witness to Socrates and to Old Comedy's freedom to insult.

## №15. Aristophanes — Lysistrata
**Annotation:** The women of Greece, led by Athenian Lysistrata, organize a sex strike and seize the Acropolis treasury to force their husbands to end the Peloponnesian War. Bodily farce turns into anti-war satire, with Spartans and Athenians reconciled by appetite.
**Historical context:** Staged 411 BC in the crisis year of the Sicilian disaster and oligarchic coup; its pan-Hellenic longing for peace makes it the most performed protest comedy in the modern repertoire.

## №16. Plato — The Apology of Socrates
**Annotation:** Socrates defends himself in court against Meletus, Anytus and Lycon: the examined life, the divine sign, refusal to flee or beg. He claims poverty as proof of integrity and warns Athens that silencing him wounds itself more than him.
**Historical context:** Delivered 399 BC after the democracy's restoration; Plato, present at the trial, shaped testimony into philosophy's founding act of conscience — the model for every later defense of free thought, from Christian apologies to dissent trials.

## №17. Plato — Crito
**Annotation:** In prison awaiting execution, Socrates refuses Crito's bribe-aided escape, arguing before the personified Laws of Athens that one must never return wrong for wrong and that a citizen who enjoyed the city's benefits may not now destroy them.
**Historical context:** Written as part of the early "Socratic" dialogues (c. 390s BC); its consent theory of obligation anticipates social-contract thinkers and frames the obey-the-law-even-unjustly problem that still divides legal philosophy.

## №18. Plato — Phaedo
**Annotation:** The death-day of Socrates in dialogue with Simmias and Cebes: arguments for the soul's immortality from opposites, recollection and kinship to the unchanging, interrupted by calm rebukes of despair and the final dose of hemlock. Philosophy practiced as rehearsal for dying.
**Historical context:** Set on the very day of the execution (399 BC) yet composed later, likely mid-380s; transmitted Pythagorean ideas into the mainstream and became the West's standard scene of the good death until Boethius.

## №19. Plato — Symposium
**Annotation:** At Agathon's victory feast, speakers climb from bodily love to love of beautiful souls, laws, knowledge and finally Beauty itself — Diotima's ladder — while drunken Alcibiades bursts in praising Socrates as the living image of that ascent.
**Historical context:** Dramatized c. 416 BC, written c. 380s; it fixed the vocabulary of Platonic love, inspired Renaissance commentaries (Ficino), and remains the central philosophical text on eros, friendship and rhetoric.

## №20. Plato — The Republic
**Annotation:** Justice in the soul and the city: the tripartite classes, philosopher-kings educated through mathematics and dialectic, the abolition of private property for guardians, the cave, line and divided city-soul analogies, and the critique of poetry that banishes the poets.
**Historical context:** Composed c. 375 BC amid the decay of Athenian democracy and Socrates' legacy; it founded political philosophy, utopian writing and epistemology alike — Popper attacked it, Karl Popper named it open society's enemy, Marx and everyone else read it.

## №21. Plato — Meno
**Annotation:** Can virtue be taught? Socrates reduces Meno to aporia, then demonstrates learning as recollection using an uneducated slave boy and geometry; at the end, virtue looks like right opinion given by god rather than knowledge — an unresolved, deliberately provocative ending.
**Historical context:** Early-middle dialogue (c. 385 BC) staging the encounter with sophistic pedagogy; its paradox of inquiry ("how will you look for what you don't know?") still drives epistemology and education theory.

## №22. Plato — Gorgias
**Annotation:** Rhetoric, says Socrates against Gorgias, Polus and the brute-calculating Callicles, is flattery like cookery; better to suffer injustice than commit it, and the unexamined political life is worthless. Callicles' might-makes-right naturalism gets the strongest answer unnamed.
**Historical context:** Written c. 385 BC when Isocratean rhetoric trained Athens' elites; the dialogue is Plato's declaration of philosophy's war on persuasion-as-power, echoing in every later critique of propaganda.

## №23. Plato — Timaeus
**Annotation:** Cosmology as "likely story": a demiurge shapes the world from a receptacle after eternal forms, weaving soul through the cosmos with mathematical ratios; the Atlantis digression frames virtuous Athens against hubristic island empire.
**Historical context:** Composed c. 360 BC; translated partly into Latin by Cicero and Calcidius, it supplied the Middle Ages and Renaissance their chief pagan account of creation, and its geometric atomism prefigured natural philosophy.

## №24. Aristotle — Nicomachean Ethics
**Annotation:** Happiness as activity of soul in accordance with virtue over a complete life; character formed by habit, choice as mean between excess and defect, friendship and pleasure analyzed, and theoretical wisdom crowned at the end.
**Historical context:** Lectures of the Lyceum (4th c. BC), edited perhaps by his son; via the Arabic commentators, Aquinas and the modern revival of virtue ethics, it remains the alternative system to Kant and utilitarianism.

## №25. Aristotle — Politics
**Annotation:** Man is by nature a political animal; the city exists for the good life, not mere living. Regimes are classified by who rules and for whom, revolution diagnosed and prevented, the middle constitution praised, slavery and education discussed without apology.
**Historical context:** Taught while Alexander tutored by Aristotle matured the imperial horizon Athens could not survive; it is the first systematic comparative politics and still structures textbooks on regimes and citizenship.

## №26. Aristotle — Poetics
**Annotation:** Tragedy defined as imitation of a serious, complete action arousing pity and fear with catharsis; plot over character, reversal and recognition, hamartia, unity of action; epic and (lost) comedy appended.
**Historical context:** Lectures c. 335 BC, recovered in the Renaissance after 1498 Latin printing; it dictated neoclassical unities for two centuries and still supplies screenwriting manuals their vocabulary of arc and turn.

## №27. Aristotle — Physics
**Annotation:** Nature as principle of motion and rest: change analyzed through matter and form, privation and the four causes; place, void, infinity, time and chance dissected; the unmoved mover introduced at the summit of the argument.
**Historical context:** Core of the Lyceum curriculum (4th c. BC); received wholesale by Islamic and medieval Latin science, it was the framework Galileo and Newton had to break — and its concept of time still provokes philosophers of physics.

## №28. Aristotle — Metaphysics
**Annotation:** First philosophy asks being qua being: substance, actuality and potentiality, form and matter, the categories, and the principle of non-contradiction defended against all comers; theology arrives as thought thinking itself.
**Historical context:** Compiled in the 1st c. BC by Andronicus from lectures (4th c. BC) whose editorial order still puzzles; it set the agenda — ousia, essence, existence — for Aquinas, Descartes, Kant and Heidegger alike.

## №29. Euclid — Elements (The primary mathematical foundation of the canon)
**Annotation:** From definitions, postulates and common notions, 13 books build number theory, geometry of magnitudes and proportion by exhaustion — including the prime infinitude, the golden section and the five solids. Proof, not measurement, is the invention.
**Historical context:** Systematized earlier results at Alexandria c. 300 BC under the Ptolemies; the most-used textbook ever written, it taught Locke, Newton and Lincoln to reason, and its axiomatic ideal governs mathematics to this day.

## №30. Cicero — On Duties
**Annotation:** A manual for his son: the honestum versus the utile, obligations of justice, beneficence, good faith, the perils of ambition, and casuistry for office-holders. Stoic morality dressed for Roman practical life.
**Historical context:** Written 44 BC after Caesar's assassination, as Cicero tried to hand republican ethics to the next generation; printed after Gutenberg as one of the first secular best-sellers, it shaped Erasmus, Enlightenment statesmen and business-ethics syllabi.

## №31. Cicero — On the Commonwealth
**Annotation:** Dialogue in Scipio's circle on the mixed constitution, justice as the bond of commonwealth, the dream of the just ruler, and the famous closing vision — the Dream of Scipio — of earth's smallness seen from the stars.
**Historical context:** Composed c. 54–51 BC in the shadow of collapsing republicanism; largely lost until 1819 palimpsest finds, its Scipionic scheme fed medieval and American ideas of balanced government.

## №32. Lucretius — On the Nature of Things
**Annotation:** Epicurean physics in hexameters: atoms and void, the soul mortal, religion as fear to be trampled, weather and earthquakes explained naturally, and the swerve freeing will — ending with the grim picture of the Athenian plague.
**Historical context:** Published c. 55 BC amid late-Republic superstition and civil strife; preserved by accident in a monastery copy rediscovered by Poggio in 1417, it electrified Renaissance materialism and modern atomic theory.

## №33. Virgil — The Aeneid
**Annotation:** Troy's refugee Aeneas carries father, gods and household to Italy, loving Dido and losing her, descending to the underworld to meet Anchises and Rome's unborn host, and killing Turnus in anger the poem weighs terribly. Pietas tested by fate and fury.
**Historical context:** Written 29–19 BC to Augustus's settlement, deliberately surpassing Homer and Ennius; it became the school-text of empire, the Christian sibyl's prophecy (Fourth Eclogue), and Dante's guide through Hell and Purgatory.

## №34. Horace — Odes
**Annotation:** Four books of lyric meters rendered in Latin: carpe diem, wine and roses, civic hymns for Augustus's moral revival, friends lost and envy dodged — irony and gravity sharing one cup.
**Historical context:** Published c. 23–13 BC under Maecenas' circle, adapting Alcaeus and Sappho to Roman idiom; its odes set the tone for two millennia of European lyric, from Ronsard to the Georgian poets.

## №35. Ovid — Metamorphoses
**Annotation:** Fifteen books of myth woven into one continuous flow: Daphne into laurel, Phaethon's fall, Arachne's web, Narcissus's pool, human ages hardening from gold to iron, ending with Caesar's star and Ovid's own immortal work.
**Historical context:** Completed AD 8, just before Augustus exiled the poet to Tomis for carmen et error; it became the Middle Ages' mythology handbook, source of Gothic sculpture, Titian, Bernini and Shakespeare's imagery.

## №36. Plutarch — Lives of the Noble Greeks and Romans (Parallel Lives)
**Annotation:** Paired biographies — Theseus/Romulus, Alexander/Caesar, Demosthenes/Cicero — compare virtue and vice through anecdote and character rather than chronology, asking how great men rose and fell and what readers should imitate.
**Historical context:** Written c. AD 100–120 under Trajan and Hadrian by a Delphic priest; Amyot's French and North's English translations made it Shakespeare's Plutarch and the founders' favorite — Jefferson's dinner-table text.

## №37. Epictetus — Discourses and Enchiridion (The Golden Sayings)
**Annotation:** What is up to us: judgment, impulse, desire; what is not: body, reputation, possessions. Adversity as training, exile as classroom, and the serenity of wanting only what the world can give freely.
**Historical context:** A freed slave lecturing at Nicopolis after Domitian expelled philosophers (c. AD 95–135); his student Arrian wrote down the Discourses and the handbook; Hadrian admired him, and Hadrian-like Marcus Aurelius proved his method livable.

## №38. Tacitus — The Annals
**Annotation:** From Tiberius to Nero: principate dissected as school of hypocrisy, informers flourishing, virtue punished, liberty flattered and betrayed — reported in a Latin of telegraphic, poisoned brevity.
**Historical context:** Composed c. AD 110–120 under Trajan and Hadrian by a consul who had served Domitian's regime; half survives, and its anatomy of unfreedom inside prosperity became the mirror held up to every autocracy since.

## №39. Marcus Aurelius — Meditations
**Annotation:** Private notebooks of an emperor on campaign: impermanence, the ruling faculty, duty to the cosmopolis, waking each morning resolved to meet interference and ingratitude without ceasing to be a human being.
**Historical context:** Written in Greek c. AD 170–180 in Danubian camps during war and plague; unpublished by design, it survived manuscripts and monasteries to become the stoic bible of William James, George Kennan and countless ordinary readers.

## №40. Ptolemy — Almagest
**Annotation:** The complete astronomy of antiquity: spheres, deferents and epicycles tabulated with trigonometric tables, star catalogue, solar and lunar theories, predictive apparatus — mathematics doing what philosophy only imagined.
**Historical context:** Compiled c. AD 150 in Alexandria; translated into Arabic as al-Majisti, it ruled Islamic observatories and medieval Europe until Copernicus answered it directly — the paradigm Kuhn chose as his example.

## №41. Plotinus — The Enneads
**Annotation:** Six sets of nine treatises ascending from sensible beauty to Soul, Intellect and the One beyond being; emanation without diminution, evil as privation, and the flight of the alone to the Alone.
**Historical context:** Lectures of a Roman teacher (AD 204–270) written down and arranged by Porphyry; baptized by Augustine's reading and by Renaissance Ficino, Neoplatonism saturated Byzantine, medieval and German Idealist mysticism.

## №42. Augustine of Hippo — Confessions
**Annotation:** Restless heart until it rests in God: stolen pears, concubine, Cicero's Hortensius, Milan garden, Monica's tears — autobiography as prayer, memory dissected, time puzzled ("what then is time?").
**Historical context:** Written c. AD 397–398 by the newly consecrated bishop of Hippo against his Manichaean past; it invented introspective confession and shaped Pascal, Rousseau and the modern memoir.

## №43. Augustine of Hippo — The City of God
**Annotation:** After Rome's 410 sack, pagans blamed Christianity; Augustine answers with two cities — of earthly love and of heavenly — reading all history as their intertwined pilgrimage toward judgment and the vision of peace.
**Historical context:** Composed c. AD 413–426 against Donatist and Pelagian controversies too; it furnished the Middle Ages its philosophy of history, and its just-war and church-state questions remain live.

## №44. Unknown Author — The Song of Roland
**Annotation:** Rearguard at Roncevaux: Roland sounds the oliphant too late, Ganelon's treason, die-hard loyalty, and Charlemagne's vengeance — feudal honor sung with blunt, hammering repetitions.
**Historical context:** Oldest major French epic, c. 1100, recited by jongleurs before crusading audiences; it codifies vassal-love and holy war, standing beside Beowulf as the charter of medieval heroic poetics.

## №45. Unknown Author — Beowulf
**Annotation:** A Geat comes to Hrotgar's mead-hall, tears Grendel's arm off, kills the monster's mother, and fifty years later dies fighting the dragon — fame measured against the transience of halls and rings.
**Historical context:** Unique manuscript c. 1000 in Old English alliterative verse preserving older Scandinavian legend; Tolkien's 1936 lecture made it scholarship, and its elegiac temper informs modern fantasy.

## №46. Thomas Aquinas — Summa Theologiae
**Annotation:** The whole of Christian doctrine ordered as questions and articles — God's existence five ways, Trinity, creation, fall, law and grace, Christ and sacraments — answering objections with surgical clarity.
**Historical context:** Written 1265–1274 after Aristotle's recovery via Albertus Magnus; left unfinished ("straw") after a mystical halt, it became Catholicism's reference text and the base of neo-Thomist revivals.

## №47. Dante Alighieri — The Divine Comedy
**Annotation:** Lost in dark wood at midlife, Dante travels guided by Virgil through nine circles of Hell, up the mountain of Purgatory, and through Paradiso's spheres to Beatrice's smile and the Love that moves the sun and other stars.
**Historical context:** Composed in Tuscan vernacular c. 1308–1320 during exile from Florence; it fixed Italian, summed medieval cosmology, and its terza rima made poetry itself a vehicle of metaphysics.

## №48. Dante Alighieri — Vita Nuova (The New Life)
**Annotation:** Prose commentary framing lyrics: first sight of Beatrice at nine, her death, visions and consolations — courtly love turned into theology of the beloved as window to God.
**Historical context:** Written c. 1292–1294 after Beatrice Portinari's death, among stilnovista experiments; it prepared the Comedy and became the template for Petrarch's Laura and all later autobiographical love-books.

## №49. Francesco Petrarch — Canzoniere (The Book of Songs)
**Annotation:** 366 poems to Laura, alive and dead: sonnets, canzoni, sestinas cycling through hope, regret, self-accusation — passion examined with a humanist's double vision, divided between earth and heaven.
**Historical context:** Worked over decades (c. 1330s–1374) by the first "modern" writer, crowned poet on the Capitol in 1415-equivalent ceremony; its Petrarchism dominated European lyric for three centuries.

## №50. Geoffrey Chaucer — The Canterbury Tales
**Annotation:** Pilgrims from knight to miller tell tales en route to Canterbury: courtly romance, fabliau, sermon, saint's life — genre colliding with class and personality in lively Middle English debate.
**Historical context:** Written c. 1387–1400 after Italian sojourns exposed Chaucer to Boccaccio; left fragmentary, it legitimized English as literary language and founded its realist, self-aware tradition.

## №51. Niccolò Machiavelli — The Prince
**Annotation:** Advice to a new prince: arms, fortune, virtù, fox and lion, cruelty well used, and the warning that it is safer to be feared than loved — politics described as it works, not as it should.
**Historical context:** Drafted 1513 after Medici restored Florence dismissed and tortured him; circulated in manuscript, printed 1532 and soon blacklisted, it split reason of state from morality and coined "Machiavellian."

## №52. Niccolò Machiavelli — Discourses on Livy
**Annotation:** Reading Rome's first decade: republics outlast princedoms, conflict of nobles and people breeds liberty, religion as civic bond, conspiracies fail, expansion corrupts — mixed constitution defended historically.
**Historical context:** Written c. 1513–1519 alongside The Prince for the same exile; adopted by 18th-century commonwealthmen and the American founders as the manual of free-statecraft.

## №53. Desiderius Erasmus — The Praise of Folly
**Annotation:** Folly herself declaims that self-deception moves the world — popes, princes, scholars, monks all nourished by flattery — a paradoxical encomium veiling sharp humanist critique of the Church.
**Historical context:** Declared c. 1509 at Thomas More's table (Moria = folly, More's name punned), printed 1511; its Greek philological program and satires prepared Reformation fault-lines it refused to join.

## №54. Thomas More — Utopian (Utopia)
**Annotation:** Travel report from nowhere: elected magistrates, no money, common property, six-hour days, toleration and slaves, religious communities — a mirror showing England its own greed reflected in an island of reason.
**Historical context:** Published 1516 in Latin after Amsterdam humanist circles; coined the genre's name, haunted by More's imminent chancellorship and martyrdom under Henry VIII.

## №55. François Rabelais — Gargantua and Pantagruel
**Annotation:** Giants born roaring: abbey of Theleme ("Do What Thou Wilt"), wine-folk, drolls, quests for the Holy Bottle — colossal appetite and learned parody satirizing scholasticism, law and war.
**Historical context:** Printed 1532–1564 under a mock-monk pseudonym amid Franciscan and Calvinist quarrels; condemned by Sorbonne, it inherited carnival's license and seeded the modern novel's polyphony.

## №56. John Calvin — Institutes of the Christian Religion
**Annotation:** Faith organized: knowledge of God and of ourselves, Scripture as lens, providence, election, depravity, church and sacraments, civil authority — systematic Protestant theology in lucid, severe Latin prose.
**Historical context:** First edition 1536, expanded till 1559 from Geneva's discipline-state; it shaped Presbyterian, Puritan and Dutch resistance cultures and Atlantic work-ethics debates.

## №57. Michel de Montaigne — Essays
**Annotation:** "I am myself the matter of my book": cannibals, coaches, thumbs, sorrow, education, friendship — judgement tested on custom and the self, scepticism lived as sociability.
**Historical context:** Retired to his tower 1571 amid France's Wars of Religion, published 1580, revised till death 1592; La Boétie's friendship anchored it, and it founded introspective prose as a genre.

## №58. William Shakespeare — Hamlet
**Annotation:** Ghost, play-within-a-play, delay, "to be or not to be": a scholar-prince tasked with revenge finds action poisoning thought, and Denmark a prison — interiority invented on the public stage.
**Historical context:** Written c. 1600–1601 from a lost earlier Hamlet and Saxo's legend; Goethe, Coleridge, Freud and Kozintsev each claimed it, and it remains the West's touchstone of consciousness.

## №59. William Shakespeare — King Lear
**Annotation:** Dividing a kingdom by flattery, Lear casts out Cordelia and finds truth in storm: nothing, folly, service and the howl of abandonment — redemption arriving too late to stop the hanging.
**Historical context:** Written c. 1605–1606 from Holinshed and an earlier King Lear; the dual-plot Quarto differs from the Folio, and its bleakness shocked Tate into a happy Restoration rewrite.

## №60. William Shakespeare — Macbeth
**Annotation:** Victors meet weird sisters, prophecy kindles murder, guilt haunts sleepless kingship — the shortest tragedy is a study of ambition's psychology and Scotland's healing.
**Historical context:** Composed c. 1606 for James I, flattering demonology and Banquo's royal lineage from Holinshed; its witch-scenes shaped centuries of performance superstition ("the Scottish play").

## №61. William Shakespeare — Othello
**Annotation:** The Moor's marriage to Desdemona poisoned by Iago's stage-managed jealousy: handkerchief, whispers, smothered innocence — racism and self-loathing exploited by motiveless malignity.
**Historical context:** Written c. 1603–1604 from Cinthio's tale; Othello's turbaned Venetian-hero status and Iago's soliloquies made it a laboratory of race, gender and manipulation criticism.

## №62. William Shakespeare — Sonnets
**Annotation:** 154 poems to a fair youth and a dark mistress: procreation urged, time defied, rivalry and lust confessed, love "not love which alters when it alteration finds" — desire argued with its own contradictions.
**Historical context:** Circulated privately 1590s, printed 1609 to Mr. W.H.; they renamed Shakespeare's biography into mystery and established the sequence as a modern lyric form.

## №63. Miguel de Cervantes — Don Quixote
**Annotation:** A gentleman reads chivalry romances till he rides out as knight-errant with Sancho Panza: windmills, galley-slaves, the barred library-burning — illusion honored and mocked at once, and the first modern novel laughs at itself.
**Historical context:** Part One 1605 after captivity at Lepanto and tax-office disgrace; Part Two 1615 answers a fake sequel by Avellaneda, inventing metafiction four centuries early.

## №64. Francis Bacon — Novum Organum
**Annotation:** Idols of tribe, cave, marketplace, theatre cleared away; induction by tables of presence, absence and degrees; heat, weight, form sought — science re-founded on patient experiment, not syllogism.
**Historical context:** Published 1620 after Lord Chancellorship's fall for bribery; its methodological manifesto fed the Royal Society and the Enlightenment's cult of useful knowledge.

## №65. Francis Bacon — New Atlantis
**Annotation:** Christians lost in Bensalem find Solomon's House: organized research, air-ships, oaths of secrecy, and commerce with revelation — techno-utopia with a pious frame.
**Historical context:** Left unfinished at Bacon's death 1626, printed 1627; it projected the research institute and inspired colonial and scientific-propaganda utopias.

## №66. René Descartes — Discourse on the Method
**Annotation:** Right use of reason: provisional morals, the cogito glimpsed mid-doubt, God's veracity guaranteeing clear and distinct ideas, mechanics of animals-as-machines — modest prose covering revolutionary demolition.
**Historical context:** Published 1637 anonymously with essays on optics, meteorology, geometry; written after the 1633 Galileo affair chilled Descartes, it opens modern philosophy's subject-centered turn.

## №67. René Descartes — Meditations on First Philosophy
**Annotation:** Six meditations: senses doubted, dream hypothesis, evil genius, I think therefore I am, wax and imagination, God's existence via perfection, error from will exceeding intellect, matter res extensa.
**Historical context:** Published 1641 with Objections from Mersenne's circle (Arnauld, Hobbes, Gassendi); it set epistemology's agenda — foundationalism, mind-body, other minds — still contested.

## №68. Thomas Hobbes — Leviathan
**Annotation:** State of nature as war of all against all; covenant authorizes the sovereign whose sword makes law, religion public, and rebellion irrational — "Mortal God" built from psychology and geometry of the passions.
**Historical context:** Published 1651 during Civil War exile under Protectorate; scandalized Anglicans and royalists alike, it remains the charter of social-contract theory and political absolutism's clearest case.

## №69. John Milton — Paradise Lost
**Annotation:** Satan's revolt, Chaos traversed, Eden tempted, Fall narrated by a blind poet invoking Heavenly Muse — freedom foreknown, "justify the ways of God to men," and heroic sin made tragically attractive.
**Historical context:** Dictated after 1660 Restoration blinded Milton lost his office and hoped regicide cause; blank-verse epic answered Renaissance romance and shaped Blake, Wordsworth, SF dystopias.

## №70. John Milton — Areopagitica
**Annotation:** Against the Licensing Order: books not dumb things, truth scattered in pieces, good and evil knowledge inseparable, licensing insults England's maturity — the founding argument for press liberty.
**Historical context:** Speech-shaped pamphlet of 1644 amid Presbyterian press controls Milton himself had used; read by Jefferson and Mill as precedent of free-market-of-ideas doctrine.

## №71. Blaise Pascal — Pensées
**Annotation:** Fragments for an Apology: greatness and wretchedness of man, the God of Abraham not philosophers, wager under uncertainty, diversion from thinking, hidden Jesus — reason brought to its knees, then comforted.
**Historical context:** Jansenist exile from Jesuit Paris, drafted c. 1656–1662 before death at 39; first published 1670 in Port-Royal edit, it became existentialism's and apologetics' shared notebook.

## №72. Baruch Spinoza — Ethics
**Annotation:** Geometry applied to divinity: one substance, God-or-Nature, infinite attributes, modes following necessarily; affects mapped — joy, sadness, desire — bondage to passion and freedom adequate understanding.
**Historical context:** Refused Amsterdam synagogue ban 1656, lens-grinder's life, finished 1675, printed posthumously 1677 and banned; it grounded German Idealism, Deleuze, Einstein's God.

## №73. John Locke — Two Treatises of Government
**Annotation:** Filmer refuted: paternal power no royal warrant; state of nature sociable, property by labor, trust forfeited by tyranny, dissolution appealing to heaven — limited constitutionalism with a right of resistance.
**Historical context:** Secretly drafted for Monmouth rising, published anonymously 1689 after Glorious Revolution; Madison and Jefferson carried its clauses into Declarations.

## №74. John Locke — An Essay Concerning Human Understanding
**Annotation:** Mind as white paper; simple ideas from sensation and reflection, complex ones compounded; primary and secondary qualities, personal identity as continuity of consciousness — innateness demolished.
**Historical context:** Begun with the Cambridge Club 1671, refined in exile, published 1690; it founded empiricism, educated Voltaire, Hume, and developmental psychology.

## №75. Isaac Newton — Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica
**Annotation:** Definitions, axioms, laws of motion and universal gravitation deducing ellipses, tides, comets, precessions — phenomena constrained by geometry, hypotheses non fingent.
**Historical context:** Prompted by Halley 1684, printed 1687 by Royal Society; it unified celestial and terrestrial mechanics, crowned the Scientific Revolution, and set the mechanistic worldview Kant would wake.

## №76. Jonathan Swift — Gulliver's Travels
**Annotation:** Lilliput's pettiness, Brobdingnag's flesh, Laputa's useless science, Houyhnhnms' cold reason and Yahoos' filth — travel satire stripping humanity's pretensions to vermin-size.
**Historical context:** Published 1726 under Lambertus Dominicus pseudonym after Whig-exile disappointments; instantly canonical, it founded children's-adventure reception while its misanthropy kept adults unsettled.

## №77. Voltaire — Candide
**Annotation:** Pangloss insists all is for the best while Lisbon quakes, auto-da-fés burn, Eldorado tempts and gardens get tilled — optimism flogged with fairy-tale velocity.
**Historical context:** 1759, anonymously, days after Seven Years' War scandals and Lisbon earthquake shattered Leibnizian theodicy; Frederick mocked it, Catherine bought copies, and "cultivate our garden" entered the language.

## №78. David Hume — A Treatise of Human Nature
**Annotation:** Ideas copy impressions; causation as custom, self as bundle, reason slave of the passions, ought-is gap, probability belief — empirical science of man pushed to skeptical conclusions.
**Historical context:** Written in La Flèche 1734–36, published 1739–40 to "dead-born" silence; it woke Kant from dogmatic slumber and anchors analytic epistemology, ethics and AI debate.

## №79. Jean-Jacques Rousseau — The Social Contract
**Annotation:** Forced to be free: general will indivisible, sovereignty inalienable, legislator godlike, civil religion tolerated — legitimate political association reconstructed from radical equality.
**Historical context:** Published 1762, burned in Paris and Geneva the same year; Jacobins worshipped it, Tocqueville and Isaiah Berlin diagnosed its totalitarian germ.

## №80. Jean-Jacques Rousseau — Emile, or On Education
**Annotation:** Negative tutoring keeps natural goodness unspoiled: senses first, things not books, adolescence's awakening (Profession of Faith of the Savoyard Vicar), Sophie for the companion — education as protection from society.
**Historical context:** Same 1762 condemnation as the Contract for its Vicar's deism; it reshaped Pestalozzi, Montessori, Dewey and every child-centered curriculum since.

## №81. Adam Smith — The Wealth of Nations
**Annotation:** Division of labour's pin factory, prices resolved into wages-profit-rent, self-interest feeding invisible supply, mercantilism unpicked, capital accumulation and history of rents — political economy's first architecture.
**Historical context:** Published 1776 after Toulouse-Bordeaux salon years and American tour; it supplied classical liberalism and Marx alike their vocabulary of value and growth.

## №82. Immanuel Kant — Critique of Pure Reason
**Annotation:** Copernican turn: objects conform to cognition; space-time pure intuitions, twelve categories, schematism, paralogisms, antinomies, ideal of pure reason — metaphysics bounded, experience saved.
**Historical context:** First edition 1781, second 1787 after Hume's alarm and Pietist schooling; it split nineteenth-century philosophy into Neo-Kantian, Idealist and positivist streams.

## №83. Immanuel Kant — Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals
**Annotation:** Good will alone unconditional; categorical imperative — universal law, humanity as end, kingdom of ends — autonomy versus heteronomy, dignity above price.
**Historical context:** 1785, between the Critique's reception and the Prussian censorship tightening; it became ethics' secular scripture and its hardest target.

## №84. Alexander Hamilton, James Madison, John Jay — The Federalist Papers
**Annotation:** Eighty-five newspaper essays urging ratification: extended republic curing faction, energy in executive, senate as check, judiciary as least dangerous branch, bill of rights unnecessary-but-eventually-added.
**Historical context:** Written winter 1787–88 in New York against Anti-Federalists; still cited by Supreme Court as authoritative original-intent commentary.

## №85. Mary Wollstonecraft — A Vindication of the Rights of Woman
**Annotation:** Against Rousseau's Émile-woman: education denied produces "spaniel timidity"; reason sexless, virtue same in women, independence sole way to respectable wives and citizens.
**Historical context:** 1792 amid French Revolution's Declaration row and her own printer-circles; it founded feminist philosophy and stung even admirers like Talleyrand.

## №86. Johann Wolfgang von Goethe — Faust
**Annotation:** Scholar-pact: Gretchen's tragedy in Part One, Helen of Troy and imperial allegory in Part Two — striving redeemed only when serving others, "the eternal feminine draws us upward."
**Historical context:** Decades-long composition (Urfaust 1770s, Part One 1808, Part Two completed 1831, published posthumously 1832); Marlowe's precursor inverted, it became Germany's national drama and musician's magnet.

## №87. Jane Austen — Pride and Prejudice
**Annotation:** Elizabeth Bennet and Darcy learn self-knowledge through misreading: wit as moral instrument, marriage market dissected without cynicism, letters and balls advancing the plot of judgement.
**Historical context:** Revised from First Impressions (1796), published anonymously 1813 amid Regency entail anxieties; it perfected free indirect discourse and the comic novel of manners.

## №88. Jane Austen — Emma
**Annotation:** Handsome, rich, self-satisfied Emma match-makes ruinously, misses Frank Churchill's game and Knightley's worth, and is instructed by Mr. Weston's tea — comedy of misunderstanding with a heroine who must grow.
**Historical context:** Written during Napoleonic blockade months, published 1815 to the Prince Regent's dedication demand; its "three or four families" canvas hid the deepest analysis of class and self-deceit.

## №89. Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel — The Phenomenology of Spirit
**Annotation:** Consciousness ascends through sense-certainty, perception, understanding, self-consciousness, master-slave struggle, unhappy conscience, reason, spirit, religion to absolute knowing — truth as the whole moving.
**Historical context:** Finished in Jena under French cannon-shot 1806, published 1807; it seeded Marx's inversion, Kojève's twentieth-century readings, and continental philosophy's historicism.

## №90. Alexis de Tocqueville — Democracy in America
**Annotation:** Journey through Jacksonian prisons and townships yields diagnosis: equality's passion, tyranny of majority, associational art, individualism soft-despotism, religion as first American institution.
**Historical context:** Volume I 1835, II 1840 after nine months' official visit 1831; it remains the classic external portrait of America and a liberal warning to Europe.

## №91. Søren Kierkegaard — Fear and Trembling
**Annotation:** Silent agonio of faith: Abraham raising Isaac above ethics — teleological suspension, the knight of faith vs infinite resignation, pseudonymous Johannes de silentio trembling at the paradox.
**Historical context:** 1843 amid broken engagement to Regine Bolette and Hegelian Copenhagen; it opened existentialism and theology's debate on divine command.

## №92. Karl Marx — Das Kapital
**Annotation:** Commodity fetishism, labor-value, surplus extracted invisibly in consumption of labor-power, primitive accumulation, falling tendency, simple reproduction schemes — capitalism anatomized from within its own categories.
**Historical context:** Volume I 1867 from British Museum desks after 1848 exile; Engels edited II–III posthumously; it split economics into schools and powered revolutions claiming its name.

## №93. Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels — The Communist Manifesto
**Annotation:** Bourgeoisie revolutionary of production, proletariat its gravedigger, specter haunting Europe, ten planks, "workers have no country" — pamphlet as compressed world-history of class struggle.
**Historical context:** Commissioned by Brussels' League of the Just, published February 1848 days before Paris rising; translated everywhere, it outlived its authors' hopes and empires.

## №94. John Stuart Mill — On Liberty
**Annotation:** Harm principle: society may coerce only self-regarding conduct exempt; tyranny of majority over taste and talk, experiments of living, individuality as utility's ingredient.
**Historical context:** 1859, year of Darwin's Origin, after Harriet Taylor's influence and Benthamite childhood breakdown; it remains liberalism's working definition of free-speech limits.

## №95. John Stuart Mill — Utilitarianism
**Annotation:** Qualities of pleasures distinguish higher/lower, rule-of-life hedonic calculus, justice as condensed utility, sanction internal conscience — utilitarianism defended against vulgarization.
**Historical context:** Sketches from 1861 Fraser's Magazine essays amid Coleridgean turn; it standardized consequentialism and provoked Sidgwick, Moore, Rawls replies.

## №96. Charles Darwin — On the Origin of Species
**Annotation:** Variation, selection, divergence, extinction's slow ledger, affinity branching tree — no teleology, no designer needed, and nature's economy red in tooth and claw quietly re-described.
**Historical context:** 1859 after twenty years' portfolio since Beagle and Wallace's letter; Within six weeks sold out, by 1872 sixth edition conceded, and biology's paradigm shifted permanently.

## №97. Herman Melville — Moby-Dick
**Annotation:** Ishmael ships out on the Pequod; Ahab's monomaniacal hunt for the white whale drags crew, Queequeg's pact, cetological digressions, and the orphan narrator's survival in coffin-life-buoy.
**Historical context:** 1851 after Polynesian voyaging and Hawthorne encouragement; initial failure, revived 1920s as American epic — whale as God, machine, obsession, text.

## №98. Fyodor Dostoevsky — Crime and Punishment
**Annotation:** Student Raskolnikov murders the pawnbroker to test Napoleon-complex arithmetic, fevers through Petersburg garrets, Sonya's reading of Lazarus, Siberian epilogue of confession and resurrection.
**Historical context:** Serialized 1866 after gambling debt and mock-execution trauma; its polyphonic psyche anticipated Nietzsche and Freud's guilty civilization.

## №99. Fyodor Dostoevsky — The Brothers Karamazov
**Annotation:** Patricide suspects Dmitri, Ivan, Smerdyakov; Elder Zosima's love-active, Grand Inquisitor poem within — faith, doubt, children's suffering, and Alyosha's stone-by-stone affirmative answer.
**Historical context:** 1879–80 final novel, months before his death; Freud called it greatest ever, Einstein read it, and its "if God does not exist everything permitted" frames modern ethics.

## №100. Leo Tolstoy — War and Peace
**Annotation:** 1805–1812 salons, Austerlitz sky, Borodino fog, Moscow fire, Pierre's Freemasonry, Andrei's death-question, Natasha's ballroom vitality — history's swarm narrated beside intimate searching.
**Historical context:** Five years' drafts after Decembrist-era research, serialized 1865–69; Tolstoy disclaimed the novel-form, and its philosophy of history annoyed historians and enraptured readers.

## №101. Leo Tolstoy — Anna Karenina
**Annotation:** "All happy families alike": Anna's train-platform entrance, Vronsky duel, Levin's mowing and agrarian doubts, Kitty's nursing — adultery tragedy crossed with provincial regeneration.
**Historical context:** 1875–77 serialization after the Sergius crisis and pedagogical experiments; Dostoevsky hailed it as European-level perfection of the psychological novel.

## №102. Mark Twain — Adventures of Huckleberry Finn
**Annotation:** Raft down Mississippi: Pap's violence, duke and king grifters, Jim's humanity, Huck choosing damnation over informing — vernacular voice exposes slavery inside boyish fun.
**Historical context:** Written 1876–83 in exile after financial shipwrecks; banned in Concord libraries 1885, rehabilitated by Hemingway's claim all American fiction flows from it.

## №103. Friedrich Nietzsche — Thus Spoke Zarathustra
**Annotation:** Prophetic parables: God dead, Übermensch, eternal recurrence, will to power, three metamorphoses camel-lion-child — philosophy performed as scripture against scripture.
**Historical context:** Parts I–IV 1883–85 after Basel professorship collapse and Lou-Salomé rupture; Wagner-refugee thought turned Wagnerian style against Wagner, influencing Mann, Kafka, Strauss tone-poem.

## №104. Friedrich Nietzsche — Beyond Good and Evil
**Annotation:** Prejudices of philosophers unmasked: will to power as interpretation, master/slave moral genealogy begun, new philosophers as commanders, danger of nationalism sniffed early.
**Historical context:** 1886 privately printed amid Sils-Maria isolation and coming collapse; sister Elisabeth later abused it for Nazism, which Nietzsche's own antisemitism-jibes disallow.

## №105. Friedrich Nietzsche — On the Genealogy of Morals
**Annotation:** Three essays: bad conscience from creditor-debt ritual, ascetic priests breeding nihilism, ressentiment flipping noble values — morality historicized with hammer-proverbs.
**Historical context:** 1887 after Paul Rée quarrel and Fouillée's reviews; it founded genealogical method for Foucault, psychoanalysis's guilt studies, and continental moral skepticism.

## №106. Sigmund Freud — The Interpretation of Dreams
**Annotation:** Wish-fulfilment disguised by censoring work: manifest/latent content, condensation, displacement, infantile wishes, Oedipus sample — unconscious given a deciphering technique.
**Historical context:** 1899 dated 1900 after Breuer's Studies on Hysteria and self-analysis; initially ignored, adopted by Rank, Jung, Jones, and surrealists, it colonized twentieth-century culture.

## №107. Sigmund Freud — Civilization and Its Discontents
**Annotation:** Libido and aggression dammed by superego's guilt; oceanic feeling questioned, happiness priced in security, sublimation, death drive — culture as tragic bargain.
**Historical context:** 1930 after World War, daughter's death, cancer; addressed to Ferenczi's ghost, it spoke to Marcuse, Fromm and every later psycho-political pessimism.

## №108. Max Weber — The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism
**Annotation:** Franklin's maxims as ethos; Luther's calling, Calvinist elect anxiety driving methodical labor, inner-worldly asceticism, iron cage of rationalized modernity — idea-causal sociology launched.
**Historical context:** 1904–05 essays after American trip and Ritschl controversy; rebutting Sombart and Fels, it became sociology's founding thesis and perpetual battleground.

## №109. James Joyce — Ulysses
**Annotation:** 16 June 1904 Dublin: Bloom's ad-agency errands, Stephen's tower regrets, Circe hallucination, Penelope's yes — Homeric scaffold under stream-of-consciousness, parody, catechism, every style.
**Historical context:** Serialized in Little Review 1918–20, obscenity prosecution halted US print until 1933 Random House; Hughes/Powers translation aid, Woolf's envy, Eliot's endorsement sealed modernism's monument.

## №110. James Joyce — A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man
**Annotation:** Baby tuck, Clongowes bullies, Christmas fallacy fight, retreats, hell-fire sermon, aesthetic theory, flight on wings — free indirect style growing with Stephen's mind.
**Historical context:** 1904–14 as Dedalus rewritten from personal notes; serial trouble in The Egoist delayed book publication to 1916 — the Künstlerroman template for the century.

## №111. Virginia Woolf — To the Lighthouse
**Annotation:** Ramsay house summers and war-gap: Time Passes bracketed decade, lighthouse reached in "Resurrection" section — consciousness, grief, painting's vision, marriage's weather patterns.
**Historical context:** 1927 after Bloomsbury and her own bout writing at Hogarth Press; Mrs Ramsay modeled on parents, Lily on herself, and its sentence-work redefined novelistic time.

## №112. Virginia Woolf — A Room of One's Own
**Annotation:** Women and fiction: £500 and lock on door, Judith Shakespeare buried unlived, Oxbridge vs women's college lunch contrast, androgyny of creative mind — essay as fiction-shaped argument.
**Historical context:** 1928–29 Newnham and Girton lectures expanded 1929; it named structural exclusion and became feminist criticism's charter sentence.

## №113. Franz Kafka — The Trial
**Annotation:** Josef K arrested without charge, attics of officials, painter Titorelli's three acquittals, cathedral parable Before the Law, throat-cut "like a dog" — bureaucracy as theological horror.
**Historical context:** 1914–15 fragments published 1925 by Max Brod against testamentary destruction order; Nazi era and Soviet show-trials made it twentieth-century's verdict document.

## №114. Franz Kafka — The Metamorphosis
**Annotation:** Gregor Samsa wakes insect-armored; family feeds him shamefully, sister's violin scene, apple lodged, death swept — alienation of wage-life rendered literally.
**Historical context:** Written November 1912 in one sitting after Felice Bauer correspondence; published 1915 Die Weisse Blätter; expressionists claimed it, and it became school-anthology staple worldwide.

## №115. Martin Heidegger — Being and Time
**Annotation:** Question of Being forgotten: Dasein's being-in-the-world, care, thrownness, fallen das Man, anxiety revealing nullity, being-toward-death, temporality as horizon — fundamental ontology.
**Historical context:** 1927 from Marburg lectures under Husserl's phenomenology, dedicated to his mentor; Sartre, Gadamer, Derrida, Merleau-Ponty all built on or against it, and its 1933 rectorate still shadows reception.

## №116. Albert Camus — The Stranger
**Annotation:** Meursault indifferent at mother's funeral, sun-struck murder on beach, trial condemning his lack of tears, cosmic indifference embraced before execution — absurd hero's diary.
**Historical context:** 1942 Gallimard launch alongside Sisyphe essay in occupied France, Algerian settler background; Sartre's "explanation" framed it, and it became absurdism's narrative signature.

## №117. Albert Camus — The Myth of Sisyphus
**Annotation:** One truly serious philosophical problem: suicide weighed against absurd divorce of man and world; Don Juan, actor, conqueror, artist as absurd exemplars, Sisyphus imagined happy.
**Historical context:** 1942 essay after Kierkegaard, Shestov, Jaspers detours and tuberculosis; it paired with Stranger to define existential-humanist option separate from Sartre's later atheism.

## №118. Jean-Paul Sartre — Being and Nothingness
**Annotation:** Pre-reflective cogito, facticity/transcendence, bad faith waiter and seduction, being-for-others shame of the Look, nothingness at heart of freedom — phenomenological ontology of human reality.
**Historical context:** 1943 written under Occupation, drawing on Heidegger, Husserl, Beauvoir's corrections; No Exit staged contemporaneously, and it armed French intellectual resistance to determinism.

## №119. George Orwell — 1984
**Annotation:** Winston rewrites Ministry of Truth, Julia's room above shop, Brotherhood bait, Room 101 rats, "loved Big Brother" — Newspeak, doublethink, telescreen surveillance engineered total control.
**Historical context:** 1948 draft on Jura against Stalin show-trials, atomic dawn, wartime MOI memories; Secker 1949, BBC serializations, and its adjectives entering daily political lexicon.

## №120. Hannah Arendt — The Origins of Totalitarianism
**Annotation:** Antisemitism's secularization, imperialism's raciology and bureau terror, then Nazism/Stalinism as mass movements making men superfluous — totalitarian novelty analyzed comparatively.
**Historical context:** 1951 after 1943–50 refugee-to-American scholarship years and Eichmann prep; banality-of-evil later developed its premises, and its "right to have rights" anchors human-rights discourse.
